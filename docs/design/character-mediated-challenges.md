# Character-mediated challenges
_Last updated: 2026-05-21_

Star-Trading uses the rule: the player chooses the strategy; the character
performs the test. The player chooses goals, methods, risk tolerance, timing,
capital exposure, fallback plans, and delegation. Character stats, traits, skill
nodes, tools, property, ship systems, staff, crew, contacts, intel, faction
standing, and preparation determine execution quality.

The five core stats are:

- `nerve`: pressure, danger, risk, intimidation, crisis control.
- `tradecraft`: deals, markets, bargaining, contacts, commercial opportunism.
- `fieldcraft`: physical systems, hazards, repair, survey, site judgment.
- `command`: leadership, delegation, staff, institutions, organized execution.
- `acumen`: accounting, valuation, forecasting, finance, ledgers, contracts,
  structured planning.

Puzzle-like interactions should create choices about approach or resource
commitment, not mandatory player-solved arithmetic, route, decoding, repair, or
tenant-management puzzles. High competency should make routine challenges
obvious, guided, cheap, low-risk, or automatic. Low competency should expose
uncertainty, hidden costs, delay, risk, failure, or the need to hire help.
